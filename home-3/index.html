Lead story · Sonua Block, West Singhbhum · June 2026

They had dropped out of school. Now they've passed Class 10.

In Sonua Block, children who came back to school through ASPIRE's residential bridge courses sat the board exams and passed. Their MP came to shake their hands.

Getting children to that exam hall is only part of our work. It starts in the Anganwadi when a child is three, and carries on after Class 10 into college, training and work. Families, teachers and village councils do it with us, in 8 tribal districts across 5 states.

§4How it works

How the model works.

We don't run our own schools. We make the government school, the Anganwadi and the village council work better, until they can keep children in school without us.

01

Access

We find children who are out of school and help them get back, and stay to the end.

More on access

02

Learning

We help children who have fallen behind catch up, and learn to keep learning on their own.

More on learning

03

Governance

We help parents, schools, and village councils run and protect education themselves.

More on governance

Explainer

What ‘child labour free’ means

A village earns the title by meeting three conditions, and keeps it only while it goes on meeting them.

  1. Every child is tracked in the Village Education Register
  2. School attendance stays above 85%
  3. An active child-rights committee (Shishu Adhikar Suraksha Samiti) watches over it

6,561 habitations hold it today.

What we believe

  1. Communities can lead. Communities have the will and the power to change things. We help them do it.
  2. Education is a public good. It is the state's job to provide it. We work to make government schools stronger.
  3. Teachers come first. Children cannot thrive if teachers are held back. We invest in teachers.
  4. Every child can grow. Given the chance, every child can reach their potential. We create that chance.
