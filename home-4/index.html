What we believe

Education changes lives. Communities sustain change.

Every child can learn, and for most children in rural India the government school is where that happens. A good school changes a child's life. It is parents, village councils and school committees who keep that change going, long after any one programme. So we work alongside them until keeping every child in school is something the whole village owns.

  1. Communities can leadCommunities have the will and the power to change things. We help them do it.
  2. Education is a public goodIt is the state's job to provide it. We work to make government schools stronger.
  3. Teachers come firstChildren cannot thrive if teachers are held back. We invest in teachers.
  4. Every child can growGiven the chance, every child can reach their potential. We create that chance.

How we work

Alongside the people already there.

We don't run our own schools. We strengthen the public system children already use, working with:

  • CommunitiesParents, village councils and school committees lead the work.
  • SchoolsGovernment schools and Anganwadis, made places worth staying in.
  • TeachersTrained and backed in their own classrooms.
  • GovernmentBlock and district officials, as partners in the public system.
  • ChildrenA voice of their own through child-rights forums.
  • 01Community-ledFamilies, village councils and school committees plan, run and protect education where they live.
  • 02School-focusedWe don't run our own schools. We strengthen the government school and Anganwadi that are already there.
  • 03System-orientedWe cover whole blocks with government, so what works can last and grow beyond our reach.
  • 04Evidence-informedEvery child is tracked in a village education register, and research partners help us learn what works.

Cradle to career

One journey, six stages.

We work at every stage in the same villages, so what a child gains at the Anganwadi is still there to build on in secondary school, and after it.

  1. Early Childhood
  2. Foundational Learning
  3. Primary Education
  4. Middle School & STEM
  5. Secondary Education
  6. Skills, Career & Livelihoods

Stories from the field

Santoshi left school after Class 4. Today she is a national medal winner.

She had stayed home to look after her younger brothers and sisters. Our field team met her during a household survey and helped her join a Girls Residential Bridge Course, which gave her a second chance at school. She went on to win bronze in archery at the 69th National School Games.
